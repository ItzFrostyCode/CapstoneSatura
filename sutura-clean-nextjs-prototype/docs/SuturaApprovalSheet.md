Introduction
Project Context
Efficiency, precision, and organization become essential for the survivalof traditional tailoring businesses in the modern fashion industry. Shop owners and designers often struggle with the administrative burden of managing manual measurements, tracking job orders, and coordinating with multiple textile suppliers. To address these challenges, many enterprises are now seeking digital solutions to refine their operations and minimize the potential for human error. Implementing automated systems for order tracking and real-time inventory oversight helps resolve production backlogs while significantly lowering administrative pressure. 
The interest in digitalize tailoring operations has surged remarkably both globally and within the Philippines. According to Global Industry Analysts (2025), the international apparel management software market reached nearly $12.8 billion in 2024. In the local landscape, the Philippine garment sector saw a 6.5 percent annual expansion, fueled by the growing popularity of bespoke fashion. These figures demonstrate that the digital evolution of the tailoring industry is becoming a vital and permanent segment of the modern service economy. As tailoring business owners focus on workflow clarity and supply chain stability, the demand for integrated systems like Sutura continues to rise.
The proponents noted that despite industry growth, the majority of small to medium-sized tailoring shops in Davao City still rely on manual processes. Many local establishments record customer measurements in handwritten notebooks, track sales and supplies through record books. Clients are often forced to visit in person or use SMS to check order progress, leading to communication gaps and misplaced data. As garment designs become more complex, these traditional methods quickly result in disorganized production schedules and longer waiting times. This lack of automation highlights an urgent need for a more structured, digital approach to professionalize the tailoring workflow.
These manual approaches create several recurring problems, such as misplaced records and the difficulty of tracking real-time fabric and thread inventory. Without a centralized system, shop owners struggle to monitor material shortages, leading to production delays and conflicts when physical receipts are lost. Managing walk-in clients alongside bulk orders for uniforms or gowns becomes equally challenging, as staff cannot efficiently prioritize tasks or access old measurement data. Consequently, these operational gaps result in disorganized schedules and frustrated customers who face unexpected wait times.
Monitoring business performance is also hindered by the use of separate physical logs, which make it difficult for owners to generate accurate reports on daily sales or tailor productivity. Tracking deposits and labor for various garment types manually often leads to financial discrepancies and disputes over lost physical receipts. According to Revillod (2024), implementing automated management systems significantly improves customer satisfaction by ensuring data accuracy and providing transparent order tracking.
Maintaining customer is also a significant challenge, as tailoring shop owners struggle to track old measurements and deposit histories across multiple handwritten notebooks. Without a digital database, manual attempts to reward loyal clients or manage long-term relationships for school and company uniform contracts become inconsistent and error-prone. According to Lantz and Hjort (2024), automated systems are essential for small enterprises to maintain service consistency and build long-term client loyalty in a competitive market.
While the system focuses on tailoring and garment production, it is built as a flexible, subscription-based platform capable of supporting diverse services of tailoring. This model allows shop owners to scale their digital tools according to their specific business needs and service categories. By providing a customization digital framework, the system ensures that local tailoring businesses can adapt to evolving market demands while maintaining a professionalized workflow.
To manage these operations effectively, the system utilizes a three-tier subscription model featuring a Basic, Pro, and Premium Plan. All plans include core functions for customer, order, appointment, inventory, and billing management to address the data loss and manual tracking issues identified in local shops. The Basic Plan provides fundamental tools like customer profile management, manual job order tracking, and a digital calendar. For expanding businesses, the Pro Plan includes all basic features plus integrated inventory management, automated notifications, and digital invoicing. The Premium Plan provides advanced features, adding multi-branch support and the ability for subscribers to customize their business profiles and showcase their specific products and garment designs in a public gallery. A centralized administrator oversees all registered tailoring centers to monitor subscription activity and ensure platform stability. This structured approach ensures that tailoring businesses can modernize their workflows while enhancing their digital presence.
With all of these challenges, from lost measurement records and inventory shortages to messy reporting and poor communication, it is clear that local tailors need a better way to manage their shops. The daily struggles observed in Davao City shops, paired with the rising demand for custom clothing, show exactly why a digital change is necessary. Because of this, the proponents are developing Sutura: A Web-Based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management. Sutura is a digital partner that brings order tracking, stock alerts, and easy reporting together in one simple place. By moving away from manual guesswork, the system aims to make life easier for shop owners, cut down on mistakes, and help our local tailoring industry truly grow and professionalize.


Purpose and Description
The purpose of this project is to provide a digital solution for tailoring businesses that still rely on manual workflows. Many shop owners currently use physical notebooks and paper logs to track measurements, job orders, and inventory. While these methods may work for small shops, they often lead to misplaced records, material shortages, and poor communication with clients. By centralizing these operations, the system aims to eliminate manual inefficiencies and help owners make better, data-driven decisions for their business. Ultimately, this study seeks to bridge the gap between traditional craftsmanship and modern technology to ensure the long-term sustainability of local tailoring enterprises.

This project introduces Sutura: A Web-Based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management to bridge existing operational gaps. Serving as a unified digital hub, the system allows owners, staff, and customers to monitor garment orders and material stocks in real time. By replacing manual notebooks with an organized database, measurement records and deposit histories become instantly accessible. Sutura provides the necessary tools to professionalize management while keeping the focus on the craft of tailoring.

Sutura functions through a tiered subscription model, allowing tailoring shops to register and select a plan that matches their specific workload. The Basic Plan provides fundamental tools like customer profile management, manual job order tracking, and a digital calendar for up to three staff accounts. For expanding businesses, the Pro Plan includes all basic features plus integrated inventory management, automated notifications, and digital invoicing for up to ten staff members. The Premium Plan offers the most comprehensive suite, adding multi-branch support, unlimited accounts, and the ability for shops to customize their profiles and showcase designs in a public gallery. 

The system organizes both walk-in and scheduled garment orders by prioritizing intake dates and tailor availability. Staff can efficiently verify measurements, assign specific tailors to tasks, and update production stages from cutting to final quality checks. Payments are processed through various digital and cash methods, with the system automatically logging deposits to prevent financial disputes. All labor costs and material usage are tracked in real time to ensure accurate productivity records for every completed order.

Sutura provides a dedicated web-based portal where they can explore garment designs and check shop availability. The platform allows clients to access their digital measurement history and monitor the live progress of their orders, from initial cutting to the final quality check. Integrated SMS and email updates ensure they stay informed with automated booking confirmations and pick-up reminders. By centralizing order tracking and communication, the system eliminates the need for manual receipts and physical follow-ups.

The system generates automated reports, such as daily sales summaries, material usage trends, and order completion rates, to facilitate informed decision-making. These analytic provide shop owners with a real-time overview of business performance, helping them optimize inventory and staffing based on high-demand garment types.

Consolidating every essential tailoring operation into a single intuitive interface allows Sutura to offer a level of organization that manual methods cannot match. This platform remains practical and scalable, specifically addressing industry needs like measurement versioning and supply chain tracking that generic software often overlooks. By integrating production monitoring, automated client updates, and multi-branch analytics, it provides a comprehensive toolkit for businesses of any size. Bridging the gap between traditional craftsmanship and modern efficiency makes the system a vital innovation for the evolving garment industry. This well-rounded digital partner ensures that shop owners can focus on their craft while the technology handles the administrative burden.

Scope and Limitations
Scope of the Study
This study covers the design and development of SUTURA: A Web-Based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management, a cloud-based platform developed using React.js, Laravel, and MySQL. The system is architected into nine (9) interconnected modules to ensure a seamless operational workflow:

1. **Subscription Module:** Manages the tiered service plans (Basic, Pro, Premium) and business registration.
2. **Customer Job & Fulfillment Order Module:** Digitizes the order lifecycle from intake and measurement versioning to cutting, sewing, and final release.
3. **Appointment Module:** Handles the scheduling and automated notifications for fittings and consultations.
4. **Inventory & Supplier Module:** Monitors raw material availability (fabrics, threads, accessories) and facilitates reordering coordination with textile providers.
5. **Admin Module:** Provides platform-level governance, tenant monitoring, and support ticket management for the system administrator.
6. **Management Module:** An administrative dashboard for Managers to monitor branch-specific analytics, financial performance, and operational oversight.
7. **Staff Operations Module:** The daily production interface where employees manage job orders, record measurements, and update production stages through a predefined access matrix.
8. **Customer Module:** A client-facing portal for order tracking, viewing measurement history, and browsing shop catalogs.
9. **Fashion Designer Module:** Allows designers to curate profiles, showcase design blueprints, and manage client consultations.

The system is intended for use by four primary user groups: platform administrators, shop managers (who oversee branch operations and business administrative tasks), tailoring staff (the employees responsible for production with predefined access levels), and clients. 

A key operational feature is the **Fixed Access Matrix for Staff**, which ensures that employees (such as Sastres, Cutters, and Quality Checkers) are automatically granted specific, non-selectable permissions relevant to their roles upon account creation. Meanwhile, the **Manager role** acts as a branch-level administrator, managing the shop’s personnel, inventory, and financial records without platform-wide ownership privileges.





For tailoring shop owners and managers, the system provides a Comprehensive Business Dashboard that centralizes critical operations. This includes a Customer and Measurement Vault for storing detailed body profiles and version-tracked measurements, alongside a Job Order Tracker that monitors every garment from initial intake through the cutting and sewing stages to final release. The platform also features an Automated Scheduling and Notification Engine, which handles fitting appointments and sends real-time SMS or email alerts to both staff and clients regarding deadlines and order milestones.
A key innovation of the system is the Inventory and Supplier Management Module. This feature allows shops to maintain real-time visibility of fabric stocks, threads, and accessories, while providing a Supplier Management Interface to track procurement history and coordinate material reordering. To ensure production continuity, the module triggers automated low-stock warnings when essential materials fall below predefined thresholds.
On the financial side, the system includes a Digital Billing and Transaction Suite that automates invoice generation and records payments including deposit tracking to maintain financial transparency. To support long-term growth, a Data Analytics and Reporting Module generates actionable insights into daily sales, material consumption trends, and tailor productivity. By consolidating these functional areas into a single web-based platform, Sutura provides local tailoring enterprises with the tools necessary to eliminate manual paperwork and optimize their entire production and supply chain lifecycle.
The study is strictly confined to software-based management and does not include hardware integration, such as automated body-scanning devices or digital measuring tools. All customer measurements must be manually encoded by authorized users. Since the platform operates as a cloud-based solution, a stable internet connection is required for all real-time updates, database access, and dashboard functionalities, as the system does not support offline data synchronization.
Functionally, the system relies on the accuracy of data provided by shop owners and staff; it cannot autonomously verify material quality, validate the physical skill level of tailors, or detect scheduling conflicts without manual input. While the platform facilitates communication, the initial version may have limited integration for automated SMS or email gateways, and online subscription payments may be restricted to manual verification or specific third-party providers rather than full API automation in the first rollout.
On the inventory and supplier coordination side, the system monitors stock levels and procurement history but does not handle the physical logistics of material delivery, such as courier tracking, shipping cost calculations, or supplier-side warehouse management. Financial tracking is limited to the digital generation of invoices and recording of transactions; the system does not perform complex tax auditing, legal compliance checks, or national-level business licensing verification.
Finally, the study is limited to the internal operational processes defined within the Sutura platform and does not extend to external management systems or third-party retail platforms beyond the provided integration modules. System performance and reliability remain dependent on server uptime and the quality of the user's internet service provider.

Review of Related Literature
This chapter presents a review of related literature, studies, and existing systems relevant to the development of SUTURA: A Web-Based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management. The review is organized into two main parts: related literature and studies, and related systems. The purpose of this review is to critically discuss what has already been studied and developed in the areas of garment production management, supply chain tracking, and customer scheduling, identify the gaps in existing knowledge and solutions, and establish why the development of Sutura is justified. Sources reviewed are identified as foreign or local where applicable.
Operational Workflows and Custom Manufacturing (Galkaduwa, 2023)
Galkaduwa (2023) explored how tailored ERP systems resolve dynamic workflow issues in custom manufacturing. By analyzing the unsuitability of conventional ERPs for small-batch sectors, the study found that manual tracking often causes bottlenecks, while specialized digital systems reduce production lead times by 30 percent and material waste by 25 percent. These gains were linked to real-time scheduling, serialized tracking, and client dashboards. While providing strong evidence that specialized technology accelerates production, the research focused on established industries like furniture and signage rather than tailoring MSMEs. This is a significant limitation because small-scale Philippine tailoring shops face unique resource constraints. Sutura addresses this gap by providing an accessible Job Order Tracker designed for local tailoring workflows, offering a more practical and integrated solution.
Supply Chain and Inventory Management (Dela Cruz & Santos, 2025) 
Dela Cruz et al. (2025) studied the Philippine micro-enterprise inventory practices, finding that manual tracking directly hinders efficiency and causes frequent stock shortages. These issues were linked to a lack of systematic tools, leading the authors to advocate for automated systems to stabilize supply chains. While providing strong evidence for digitalization, the study focuses on general practices rather than the complex materials management like fabrics, buttons, zippers and etc that is required in tailoring. This is a significant limitation since tailoring business is inseparable from specific job orders. Sutura addresses this by integrating a specialized Supplier Management module that tracks materials relative to client orders, providing a more complete, localized solution than manual methods.
Personalized Communication and Strategic Retention (Chhabria & Lasola, 2025) 
Chhabria and Lasola (2025) analyzed personalized engagement in the Philippine fashion sector, finding that hyper-personalized interactions and structured customer relaionship management practices significantly increase repeat visits. Customers reported higher satisfaction when systems remembered their specific preferences, creating value beyond the service itself. The studies concluded that proactive communication gives businesses a measurable advantage in reducing churn. However, the research did not examine how these strategies could be automated within a market segment system. This is a significant limitation because manual tracking in local tailoring shops is prone to inconsistency and error. Sutura closes this gap by providing a centralized client profile and automated notification system that tracks preferences and updates status after every job order, removing the manual follow-up burden entirely.

Review of Related Systems
The Tailor Works is a premier online custom tailoring platform designed to provide high-end, made-to-measure clothing services directly to customers. It is a service-rich system that offers personalized home and virtual consultations, allowing clients to collaborate with expert designers on custom outfits from any location. The platform manages specialized bespoke collections, ranging from wedding attire and maternity wear to gender-diverse clothing, ensuring a perfect fit through a digital-first approach. Beyond basic tailoring, the system supports premium services such as upcycling, professional styling, and intricate alterations. For operational efficiency, the platform streamlines the design process by enabling designers to bring fabric swatches to clients and finalize designs on-site. The Tailor Works also includes corporate branding solutions and a dedicated Launch Pad for other designers, facilitating a quick turnaround time and a seamless connection between traditional craftsmanship and modern convenience.
 
Despite these strengths, The Tailor Works comes with limitations that reduce its suitability for many general tailoring businesses. Its premium service model is built around high-end luxury, which results in a higher price point that can be costly for budget-focused shops or value-conscious clients. The platform is also heavily dependent on geography, as its doorstep service requires expert designers to be physically present in specific regions, limiting its accessibility in areas where a local workforce is not yet established. It does not include a platform-level administrator dashboard for overseeing multiple independent tailoring businesses at a system-wide level. Simple, high-volume repair tracking is also not part of its core functionality, making it less practical for community shops that do not require a full designer consultation for every task.
 
SUTURA emerges as the more effective solution for local tailoring businesses. While The Tailor Works focuses on luxury doorstep services, SUTURA provides an all-in-one management platform that handles walk-ins, material tracking, and bespoke measurements in a single dashboard. Our system includes real-time production monitoring and multi-shop administration without the high costs of a premium designer-led model. It bridges the gap between high-end customization and daily shop efficiency, offering tools that prioritize the tailor’s workflow. For businesses seeking a practical and comprehensive digital partner, SUTURA is the superior choice.

Tom Tailor is one of Europe’s leading fashion and lifestyle brands, operating in over 30 countries with a focus on high-quality, casual wear for a broad global audience. It offers a diverse range of collections through its primary labels, Tom Tailor and Tom Tailor Denim, which cater to both timeless styles and modern, trend-focused in street wear. Customers can access their products through a vast network of physical stores, franchises, and major online marketplaces like Zalando. The brand provides versatile essentials that work for everyday life, whether for school, work, or leisure. Furthermore, Tom Tailor incorporates a be part sustainability initiative into its business model, which formalizes its commitment to ethical labor, environmental protection, and industrial progress. Tom Tailor also integrates sustainability into its business model by focusing on recycled materials and supply chain traceability. For modern families and young professionals, the brand serves as a reliable source for durable, comfortable clothing that balances quality with an approachable, mid-range price point.
 
However, Tom Tailor has clear limitations that restrict its utility for specialized tailoring services. Its mid-price positioning creates margin pressure against ultra-fast fashion retailers with lower prices and faster inventory rotations. Operationally, the system is designed to manage only the finished goods, tracking garments by the box rather than monitoring the specific stages of production. Because it relies on mass-market standard sizing, the platform lacks the capability to store individual, detailed body measurements for personalized fittings. Furthermore, while the system tracks inventory through garment barcodes, it does not account for raw materials like fabric yardage or small components such as specific buttons and zippers. 
 
SUTURA goes well beyond the capabilities of retail-focused systems like Tom Tailor. While those platforms only handle finished goods, SUTURA integrates custom measurement tracking, raw material management, and real-time production monitoring into one central dashboard. This allows tailors to oversee the entire garment-making process from tracking fabric yardage to the final fitting ensuring total precision for every custom order. By simplifying multi-shop administration without the headache of mass-market logistics, SUTURA empowers independent businesses to scale efficiently. Ultimately, it offers a more personal and technical approach for shops that prioritize quality craftsmanship over mass production.

Alterations Plus is a leading professional garment care and clothing modification service provider in the Philippines, operating extensively through mall-based branches. It serves as a comprehensive one-stop shop for diverse clothing needs, specializing in precise alterations, repair services, and custom-made apparel for both individual and corporate clients. The platform is designed for high-volume efficiency, offering specialized services such as embroidery, dry cleaning, and invisible mending to restore damaged fabrics. Its business model focuses on accessibility and reliability, utilizing a standardized workflow across its many physical locations to ensure consistent quality and quick turnaround times. Beyond simple fixes, Alterations Plus also provides bespoke tailoring for formal wear and uniforms, catering to a broad market that ranges from walk-in retail customers to large-scale institutional contracts.
 
Beyond these strengths, Alterations Plus has limitations that restrict its operational depth for independent tailoring management. Because the system is optimized for high-volume retail traffic in malls, it lacks integrated tools for managing off-site home service appointments or virtual designer consultations. The platform primarily functions on a branch-by-branch basis, without a unified dashboard that allows independent shop owners to monitor raw material inventory, such as specific fabric yardage or specialized hardware, across various suppliers. Additionally, while it handles standardized repairs efficiently, the system is not built to store and track highly personalized, long-term body measurement profiles for bespoke tailoring clients over time.
 
SUTURA stands out as the more versatile and technically grounded choice for modern tailoring businesses. While Alterations Plus excels in mall-based retail repairs, SUTURA provides an all-in-one platform that bridges the gap between simple alterations and complex bespoke production. Our system integrates real-time production monitoring, detailed measurement archives, and raw material tracking into a single, easy-to-use dashboard. By offering smart queue management for both walk-ins and home services, SUTURA ensures a seamless workflow that traditional retail systems cannot match.

Synthesis
	The reviewed literature emphasizes that the primary driver of inefficiency in custom tailoring is the reliance on fragmented, manual workflows. Galkaduwa (2023) established that generic tools fail small-batch manufacturing, whereas specialized digital tracking significantly reduces lead times and waste. This is reinforced by Dela Cruz and Santos (2025), who found that manual inventory in Philippine micro-enterprises leads to stock shortages that only automated supply chain tools can resolve. Furthermore, Chhabria and Lasola (2025) proved that business sustainability in the local fashion sector requires automated customer management to accurately track unique preferences and measurements.
The reviewed systems, The Tailor Works, Tom Tailor, and Alterations Plus, each serve specific market segments well, but none deliver a comprehensive operational solution for independent tailors. The Tailor Works excels in luxury doorstep service but is geographically limited and lacks high-volume, standardized repair tracking. Tom Tailor efficiently manages global mass-market retail but only tracks finished garments, omitting the raw material inventory and personalized body measurements required for custom work. Alterations Plus dominates mall-based fast repairs but operates on a fragmented branch level, lacking support for off-site home services and long-term bespoke client profiling. Ultimately, none of these existing systems provide a unified multi-shop administrator dashboard, stage-by-stage production monitoring, or detailed raw material tracking.
 
These specific operational gaps, highlighted across both literature research and the existing systems are precisely what SUTURA is engineered to resolve. By unifying stage-by-stage production monitoring, detailed raw material tracking, automated long-term client measurement profiling, and multi-shop administration into a single centralized dashboard, the system effectively eliminates the persistent inefficiencies of manual workflows. Furthermore, by seamlessly accommodating both high-volume standardized repairs and personalized off-site home service bookings, SUTURA delivers a technically comprehensive and highly adaptable management solution that empowers independent tailoring businesses far beyond the capabilities of existing software.

Technical Background
Technologies to be Used
The development of Sutura: A Web-Based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management will utilize modern, accessible, and industry-relevant technologies aligned with current standards in web application development. The system will adopt a client-server architecture, enabling centralized data management and multi-user access for tailoring shop owners, staff, and suppliers through a single unified platform. The system will be fully responsive, ensuring optimal usability across desktop computers, tablets, and mobile browsers without requiring a separate mobile application. 
For the frontend, the system will use React.js, a component-based JavaScript library developed by Meta, to build a dynamic, structured, and user-friendly web interface for administrators, shop owners, and staff. React.js will be paired with Tailwind CSS, a utility-first CSS framework, to ensure a clean, professional, and fully responsive layout that automatically adapts to different screen sizes including desktops, tablets, and smartphones. Together, these technologies eliminate the need for a separate mobile application while still delivering a consistent and accessible user experience across all devices and screen resolutions. 
On the backend, the system will utilize Laravel, a PHP-based web application framework, to manage all server-side operations including business logic, user authentication, role-based access control, and data processing. Laravel will expose RESTful APIs to facilitate seamless communication between the frontend interface and the server, ensuring efficient and real-time data exchange across all system modules such as order tracking, inventory monitoring, and supplier management. 
For data storage, MySQL will serve as the primary relational database management system, providing a structured and reliable schema for managing customer profiles, body measurements, job orders, inventory records, supplier information, billing records, and transaction history. MySQL is well-suited for Sutura's operations as it supports structured relational data, which is essential for linking customer orders to inventory consumption and supplier procurement records. 
The database will be hosted on PlanetScale, a free and scalable cloud-based MySQL hosting platform, ensuring data availability, security, and remote accessibility without the need for on-premise server infrastructure. 
For deployment, the frontend React.js application will be hosted using Vercel, a free cloud hosting platform optimized for modern web applications, while the Laravel backend and its API services will be deployed on Railway or Render, both of which offer free-tier cloud hosting suitable for academic capstone deployment. These platforms ensure that the system remains accessible online throughout the development and testing phases of the project. 
For the development environment, the team will use Visual Studio Code as the primary code editor due to its extensive support for PHP, JavaScript, and the frameworks used in this project. 
GitHub will be utilized for version control and collaborative development, allowing team members to manage, track, and merge code changes efficiently throughout the development lifecycle. 
Postman will be used to test and validate all RESTful API endpoints, ensuring system reliability, data integrity, and consistent performance before integration. 
Additionally, Figma will be used for designing user interface wireframes and interactive prototypes prior to development, serving as the visual and structural blueprint for all system screens, user flows, and design components. 
Hardware Requirements The development and deployment of Sutura will require the following minimum hardware specifications. For the development environment, each team member's workstation must be equipped with at least an Intel Core i3 processor or its equivalent, a minimum of 4 GB RAM, and at least 256 GB of available storage to support the installation and operation of all required development tools and local testing environments. A stable broadband internet connection is required for cloud database access, GitHub version control operations, and online deployment activities. 
For end users, any standard desktop computer, laptop, tablet, or smartphone equipped with a modern web browser such as Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Microsoft Edge is sufficient to access and operate the Sutura system. Since the web application is designed to be fully responsive across all screen sizes and devices, no additional hardware or software installation is required on the user's end beyond an active internet connection and a compatible web browser.

Requirements Analysis
Within the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), the requirements analysis phase is essential for pinpointing and establishing the precise needs of both the users and the software. For the proposed system, Sutura: A Web-based Subscription Tailoring Business Management System with Supplier Management, this stage concentrates on eliminating the bottlenecks associated with traditional, manual tailoring workflows by designing a unified, automated digital platform.
 
The system involves four main users: the system administrator, tailoring shop owners or managers, the tailoring staff, and the customers. The administrators manage the platform subscriptions and user access. Shop owners or managers oversee business analytics, inventory, and staff assignments. Tailoring staff handle measurements and production workflows, and customers can browse shops, place orders, and track garment progress in real-time.
 
The system operates within a web-based environment accessible through standard web browsers. Shop owners and tailoring staff primarily access the system via desktop interfaces for comprehensive management and data entry, while the platform features a mobile-responsive design for customers. This allows clients to browse garment designs, submit tailoring requests, and monitor their order progress remotely from their mobile devices. The system is specifically designed for use within the physical premises of registered tailoring shops in Davao City, as well as remotely by their respective clients.
 
The system must support key functional requirements, including a real-time ordering system and automated production tracking. Timing and triggers are central to the system’s operation: notifications are triggered instantly when an order is placed or when a garment moves to a new production stage, such as cutting or final fitting. Furthermore, business reports and analytics are generated automatically at the end of each day or upon request, providing shop owners with immediate insights into their sales and inventory levels. The platform also facilitates comprehensive supplier tracking alongside internal task assignments. It supports flexible billing through cash and digital gateways like GCash and Maya, integrated with a SaaS-based subscription module for efficient operation management.
Non-functional requirements include reliability, security, usability, and scalability. The system shall have 99.9% uptime and perform automatic database backups every 24 hours, protect sensitive information, and ensure an intuitive experience for all users. It is deployed in a cloud-based environment to provide high availability and real-time synchronization, allowing the system to handle multiple tailoring enterprises in Davao City efficiently.
 
In conclusion, this analysis ensures that Sutura is strategically aligned with the users' needs and the specific working environment of the local tailoring industry, providing a strong roadmap for the subsequent design and technical implementation of the platform.

























Requirements Documentation
	The Requirements Documentation serves as the definitive guide for building the Sutura platform. It acts as a formal agreement between the developers and the participating tailoring shop stakeholders. This document explicitly details both the functional and non-functional capabilities the software must possess.
 
The system shall provide core functionalities including secure user authentication and role-based access for administrators, shop owners, tailoring staff, and customers. It must support a centralized digital hub that enables real-time job order tracking, measurement storage, and automated fitting appointment scheduling. An integrated inventory and supplier management module will actively monitor fabric and material stocks, facilitate coordination with textile providers, and trigger automated low-stock alerts when materials require reordering. Additionally, the platform shall feature financial tracking capabilities to streamline invoice generation and produce comprehensive business analytics.
 
The system will feature a flexible billing module supporting cash and digital payments like GCash and Maya, with all transactions logged automatically for transparency. A SaaS-based subscription module will enable shop owners to register and manage tiered accounts seamlessly. Additionally, the platform will generate automated business analytics, such as sales and inventory reports, to provide data-driven operational insights.
 
Regarding non-functional requirements, the system ensures high reliability and data integrity through its cloud-based architecture, which minimizes system errors and prevents tracking discrepancies. Security measures are implemented to protect sensitive user information and secure financial transactions processed through integrated digital gateways. The interface is specifically designed for high usability, providing an intuitive experience for both technical staff and non-technical customers. Furthermore, the platform is built for scalability to efficiently handle multiple tailoring enterprises and a growing user base without performance degradation.


In summary, this documentation provides a clear and detailed foundation for the architectural design and development of the Sutura system. It ensures that all essential functional features and non-functional qualities are explicitly defined and strategically aligned with user needs.
